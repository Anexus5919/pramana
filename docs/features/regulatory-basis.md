# Regulatory basis: "Relates to" and "What must be established"

Every finding an official opens now says which provisions it concerns and what has to be established before anyone acts on it. Officers, verifiers and admins see it; institutions do not. Pramana relates a finding to the text. It does not say a provision is breached, does not call a document anything, and does not decide: the competent authority does.

Code: `backend/rulesets/regulatory_basis.yaml` (the map), `backend/pramana/rules/basis.py` (Pydantic model, resolution, cache), `backend/pramana/api/applications.py` (`with_basis`), `backend/pramana/officer/report.py` and `backend/pramana/insights/inspection.py` (one "Relates to" line), `frontend/src/pages/application/sections/findings/RegulatoryBasis.tsx` (the two sections), `backend/scripts/verify_regulatory_basis.py` (re-checks every quote against the PDFs). Tests: `backend/tests/test_regulatory_basis.py`.

## 1. What the official sees

In the finding panel, "Relates to" sits after the Rule block and "What must be established" after the evidence, before the history:

- **Relates to.** One line under the heading: "Provisions this finding concerns. Whether one is breached is for the competent authority." Then one card per provision: the instrument and its number ("Approval Regulations, 2020 · Clause 9.3"), the verbatim quote in quotation marks, and "Source, p. N", which opens the official PDF at that page in a new tab. The order is handbook, Regulations, Act, then what the texts provide when information or documents are found false.
- **What must be established.** A numbered checklist for the official before acting. Each step carries a small tag: the provision that provides for it ("Regulation 11.2", "APH cl. 2.4", linked to its page), or "Evidence" when the step is evidentiary good practice rather than a requirement of the text.

For a rule finding, the rule's own handbook clause stays in the Rule block above, so "Relates to" lists the related provisions rather than repeating it. For a finding about one document (forensic, verification, cross-application, photo), the handbook clause that requires that document type comes first: a finding on the fire certificate relates to APH Clause 6.17(i) and Affidavit 2 item 7.

Example, A7 (fire certificate whose validity date was edited after signing), finding "Fire Safety Certificate: mismatch detected (issuer signature)":

- Relates to: APH Clause 6.17(i); APH Affidavit 2 item 7; APH Annexure 16.8 (Scrutiny Committee: "Verify the authenticity of the documents..."); AICTE Act Section 10(k); Regulations Clause 9.3 (information or documents found false); APH Clause 7.4(a).
- What must be established: obtain the issuer's original certificate or its written confirmation (Evidence); confirm the printed value differs from what the issuer signed or holds (Evidence); rule out an ordinary cause such as a comment or a re-save (Evidence); give the institution an opportunity to explain and to be heard (Regulation 11.2); record the decision with reasons (Regulation 7.4).

The evidence pack's report.pdf and the inspection brief carry one line under each finding or question: "Relates to: APH cl. 6.17(i); APH Affidavit 2 item 7; ...".

No criminal law is cited. The handbook and the Regulations do contain such language (APH Clauses 1.4.4, 2.3.4(b), 2.3.5(b), 7.1(a) and 7.2; Regulations Clauses 9.3, last sentence, and 11.1). Those sentences are deliberately left out, and no quote in the map contains them. Nothing refers a matter anywhere.

## 2. Who gets it, and payload

- `GET /api/applications/{id}/findings` and `POST /api/findings/{id}/review` return `regulatory_basis` for officer, verifier and admin. Institutions get the same finding list as before, without it; the clause they must meet reaches them in the deficiency letter.
- Shape: `{entry, scope_note, version, sources: {APH: {name, url}, ...}, provisions: [{id, source, number, cite, quote, page, verified}], establish: [{text, basis: {kind: "evidence"} | {kind: "provision", label, id, source, page}}]}`. Each instrument's name and URL appear once per finding, under `sources`.
- Resolution is by finding code and the declared type of the finding's document, from an in-memory map that is validated once and cached (`lru_cache`), so a list costs one extra query for document types. About 3 KB per finding, almost all of it the quotes.
- The map is validated with Pydantic when the server starts, so a broken map stops the server rather than a finding page. Every reference must resolve; an entry must have provisions and steps; a code or pattern may be mapped once only; a provision must be `verified: true`; and no quote, number, step or title may contain accusing or criminal-law vocabulary (the pattern is `FORBIDDEN` in `basis.py`).
- `active_ruleset()` now skips `regulatory_basis.yaml`, which sits beside the ruleset but is not one.

## 6. Limits

- **Addenda not retrieved.** The Addendum/Corrigendum 2025-26 and the 2026-27 (V2) addendum could not be fetched from AICTE (site under maintenance) or from any government host that served them on 27 Oct 2026; the only copies found were behind unreachable state sites. No addendum provision is cited. The rules that cite the 2026-27 addendum (`ADD26-26C-CORE-BRANCHES`, `XR-NBA-CLAIMS`) still show the ruleset's addendum quote in their Rule block; "Relates to" cites the base handbook Clause 2.6(c) and Regulation 6.3(f) as substituted in 2021 instead.
- **Public Notice 2026-27 and the portal user manual** (cited by `PN2627-DEADLINE` and `XD-AFF2-APPLICATION`) were not retrieved. Those findings relate to APH Clause 1.4.3, Regulation 5.6(b) and the handbook's affidavit note, which were verified.
- **Criminal-law language left out** by product rule (section 1). The Regulations' and handbook's own penalty provisions are cited only where their quoted text carries no such language.
- **Hindi text not used.** Both Gazettes are bilingual; the Hindi pages use a legacy font whose text layer cannot be read, so only the English text was quoted.
- **Mirrors.** The handbook copy is from a state government host and the Act from an archive capture, as described in section 3. Their identity with AICTE's and India Code's files rests on the cross-checks recorded there, not on a download from those sites today.
- **Quote length.** "A sentence or less": a few quotes are one long sentence (Regulation 9.3, 11.2) or a contiguous fragment of one (APH Clause 7.4(a), Regulation 5.6(a)). Fragments are cut, never edited; no ellipsis is used inside a quote.
- **Granularity.** The map is keyed by code and document type, not by the facts of a case. "Opportunity to be heard" is tagged with Regulation 11.2, which provides an inquiry by the Standing Hearing Committee and a hearing by the Standing Appellate Committee before approval is withdrawn; whether that route applies to a particular decision is the official's call.
- **Handbook quotes cited by the rules themselves** were also checked, as information (`--ruleset`): 24 of 29 rules cite the handbook, and 19 of those quotes appear verbatim on their cited page. Five are condensed or use different quote marks and are worth a follow-up in the ruleset: `XR-INTAKE-RECORD` (single for double quote marks), `XD-OC-INSTITUTION` and `XD-FIRE-INSTITUTION` (sentence cut before "(as applicable)" and ended with a full stop), `APH-ANX5-FSR` (a table summarised as a sentence) and `APH-ANX33-ADMIN-AREA` (two sentences from Annexures 3.3 and 3.4 joined). The map quotes the corresponding clauses verbatim where it cites them.
